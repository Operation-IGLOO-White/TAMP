TAMP Matchmaking Process

This document explains how TAMP identifies compatible loads and trucks, how either party may initiate an engagement, and how mutual acceptance becomes an operational trip.

Core principle: A match is not a job assignment. TAMP requires agreement from both the Cargo Owner and Truck Owner before a trip is created.

1. Purpose and Scope

The matchmaking process connects a verified Cargo Owner's posted load with a verified Truck Owner's available vehicle. It covers:

  • Mandatory eligibility and compliance checks;
  • Transparent scoring and ranking of eligible trucks;
  • Cargo Owner-initiated and Truck Owner-initiated engagements;
  • Mutual confirmation, reservation, dispatch and driver assignment; and
  • Notifications, expiry and audit records.

2. Parties

Cargo Owner

Creates the load, reviews matching trucks, accepts a suggested truck or approves a carrier request.

Truck Owner

Maintains available trucks, reviews matching loads, requests work or confirms a Cargo Owner's selection.

3. Preconditions for Matchmaking

A load and truck enter matchmaking only when the following foundational conditions are satisfied.

AreaCargo Owner / LoadTruck Owner / Vehicle
IdentityOrganisation verified and active.Carrier organisation verified and active.
Operational stateLoad is posted and not cancelled, completed or committed.Truck status is Available and it is not reserved or on another trip.
CapacityWeight and cargo requirements supplied.Payload capacity is equal to or greater than load weight.
EquipmentSuitable body types are determined from cargo type.Truck body type is suitable for the cargo.
SchedulePickup window and delivery time supplied.Truck availability overlaps the pickup schedule.
ComplianceRequired owner documents remain valid.Vehicle licence, compliance documents and required insurance remain valid.
LocationValid pickup coordinates selected.Current truck location is valid and within the configured maximum radius.

4. Matching Engine Sequence

1
Load is publishedTAMP records the route, cargo, weight, body-type requirements and schedule.
↓
2
Candidate trucks are collectedOnly currently available trucks are considered.
↓
3
Hard filters are appliedCapacity, body type, schedule, radius and compliance rules must pass. A hard-filter failure cannot be repaired by a high score.
↓
4
Eligible trucks are scoredEach qualifying truck receives a transparent score out of 100.
↓
5
Suggestions are rankedEligible matches meeting the configured minimum score are presented as actionable opportunities.
Default suggestion threshold: 70/100. The Platform Administrator may change this value in Matching Engine Controls. The same live configuration must be used on both Cargo Owner and Truck Owner screens.

5. Match Score

The score ranks trucks that already passed every mandatory safeguard. The standard score has five components:

ComponentWhat it measuresMaximum
Pickup proximityDistance between the truck's current position and load collection point.30
Capacity fitWhether the truck can carry the load and how efficiently its payload is utilised.25
Pickup timingOverlap between truck availability and the pickup window.20
Preferred laneAlignment between the truck's saved lane/destination and the load route.15
Carrier ratingThe carrier's verified platform rating and operating history.10
Total100
MATCH SCORE = PROXIMITY + CAPACITY + TIMING + LANE + RATING

Timing example

If a truck is available from 06:00–10:00 and the pickup window is 09:00–13:00, the common time is 09:00–10:00: one hour.

TIMING POINTS = 20 × (OVERLAP HOURS ÷ PICKUP-WINDOW HOURS)
20 × (1 ÷ 4) = 5 TIMING POINTS

Example shown in Fleet Register

A truck may display a best matching load with a score of 67.97/100. This means the truck passed the hard filters and this is its best current load, but it is 2.03 points below the default automatic suggestion threshold of 70.

Required user experience: The card must explain why it is not actionable. Example: “Score 67.97 — requires 70. Improve proximity, availability or preferred lane.”

6. Match Visibility

ResultSystem behaviour
Hard filter failedNot actionable. The failure reason should be visible to the authorised user.
Eligible but below thresholdMay be shown as an informational best match, with the score gap explained.
Eligible and at/above thresholdShown as an actionable suggestion to the relevant party.

7. Process A — Cargo Owner Initiates

1
Cargo Owner posts the loadStatus becomes Posted.
↓
2
TAMP generates suggested trucksThe Cargo Owner reviews score, route, vehicle, capacity, compliance and carrier information.
↓
3
Cargo Owner selects “Accept Match”The selected truck is proposed to its owner. This does not yet create a trip.
↓
4
Truck Owner reviews the proposalThe proposal appears under Engagements → Awaiting Your Confirmation.
↓
5
Truck Owner confirms or declinesConfirming records the carrier's agreement to the same load, truck, schedule and rate revision.
↓
6
Trip is createdStatus becomes Confirmed. The truck is reserved and the trip moves to Dispatch.

If the Truck Owner declines

  • The decline reason is recorded in the audit trail.
  • The load remains open if its schedule has not expired.
  • The Cargo Owner may review another eligible truck.
  • No trip or driver assignment is created.
Control: A Cargo Owner selecting a truck is an invitation to engage, not unilateral dispatch authority.

8. Process B — Truck Owner Initiates

1
Truck Owner keeps a truck availableThe current location, payload, body type, availability and preferred lane are maintained in Fleet Register.
↓
2
TAMP displays matching loadsActionable opportunities meeting the configured minimum appear under Engagements → Job Opportunities.
↓
3
Truck Owner selects “Request Job”An Offered request is recorded and sent to the Cargo Owner. No trip exists yet.
↓
4
Cargo Owner reviews the carrier requestThe Cargo Owner reviews the truck, score, compliance, rate and route, then approves or rejects it.
↓
5
Truck Owner confirms the approved engagementThe carrier confirms the final accepted revision so neither party is bound to changed terms without review.
↓
6
Trip is createdThe load and truck become committed and the job enters Dispatch.

Request controls

  • A truck may request only while it remains Available.
  • Duplicate active requests for the same load and truck are prevented.
  • A request expires after the configured response period (currently designed as 24 hours).
  • The Truck Owner may withdraw a pending request before Cargo Owner approval.
  • Rejected or expired requests do not reserve the truck.
Fleet Register improvement: “Best Matching Load” should provide View Match and Request Job actions when eligible, and a clear disabled explanation when below the live threshold.

9. Match and Trip Statuses

StatusMeaningNext responsible party/action
PostedLoad is open for matching.Either party may initiate an eligible engagement.
SuggestedSystem-generated candidate; no party has committed.Cargo Owner may accept, or Truck Owner may request.
OfferedTruck Owner requested the job.Cargo Owner approves or rejects.
AcceptedOne party accepted; final carrier confirmation is outstanding.Truck Owner confirms the reviewed engagement.
ConfirmedBoth parties agreed. A trip exists.Truck Owner assigns a verified driver.
ScheduledTrip is ready for dispatch or awaiting driver acceptance.Dispatcher and driver.
In TransitDriver started and GPS/trip events are active.Driver progresses the trip; both organisations monitor.
DeliveredDelivery and proof-of-delivery workflow completed.Settlement, rating and closure.

10. Notifications and Audit

  • Cargo Owners receive notifications for new carrier requests, confirmations, declines and trip events belonging to their organisation.
  • Truck Owners receive notifications for accepted trucks, engagement approvals, expiring offers and driver responses belonging to their organisation.
  • Drivers receive only job offers and trips assigned to them.
  • Every request, acceptance, rejection, confirmation, withdrawal and expiry records the actor, organisation, date/time and relevant identifiers.

11. Acceptance Checklist

✓Both initiation paths are available and understandable.
✓No trip is created until both parties agree.
✓The same administrator-configured threshold is used on every screen.
✓Below-threshold and hard-filter failures show user-friendly reasons.
✓Confirmed work reserves the truck and moves to Dispatch.
✓Notifications and data remain restricted to the relevant organisation.
Summary: TAMP is a bilateral marketplace. The engine recommends; the Cargo Owner and Truck Owner decide; Dispatch begins only after mutual confirmation.
